YouTube Thumbnail Checklist

Fifteen questions to ask about every thumbnail before you hit publish. Tick what's true, and the checklist scores your thumbnail and tells you what to fix first. Your answers are saved in your browser.

1. The idea

If the idea needs a paragraph, the thumbnail will be confusing at a glance. Simplify to one promise.

Let the image show and the title tell. Repeating the title in the image wastes space.

Misleading thumbnails cause early drop-off and can violate YouTube's policies.

2. Readability at small size

That's roughly the size in the desktop "Up next" column. Check it in the feed preview.

For example a face, an object and a few words. Every extra element competes for attention.

Heavy sans-serif font, large, with a stroke, shadow or block behind it. See the typography guide.

The video-length badge covers it.

3. Contrast and color

The grayscale test catches most contrast problems. The Thumbnail Analyzer shows it automatically.

Light mode and dark mode. Watch for edges that disappear.

Too many competing colors make the image harder to read. See the color guide.

4. Emotion and story

No face? Tick this if the subject itself carries the emotion or tension. See faces in thumbnails.

Curiosity that the video pays off, not a bait-and-switch.

5. Technical and context

Limits: 50 MB from a computer, 2 MB from the mobile app. See the size guide.

Search your topic and compare. Blending in makes you invisible.

Consistent enough to be recognized, different enough to be noticed as new.

    How the score works

    Each item has a weight from 1 to 3 based on how much it usually affects a thumbnail's clarity and honesty. The items that matter most — a single clear idea, readability at small size, contrast in grayscale, and a video that delivers on the promise — count three times as much as small technical details. The score is simply the weighted share of items you've ticked.

    It's a self-review, not a prediction. A thumbnail that scores 100% can still underperform if the topic isn't interesting to your audience, and the only reliable measure of performance is real viewers. Once your thumbnail passes the checklist, compare variants with YouTube's Test & Compare.

    Using the checklist in your workflow

    1. Design two or three options rather than one. It's easier to judge thumbnails side by side.
    2. Run the measurable checks with the Thumbnail Analyzer (contrast, clutter, edges, specs).
    3. Go through this checklist for the judgment calls software can't make.
    4. Preview the best options in context with the feed preview.
    5. Publish and test the top two with Test & Compare.

    For the reasoning behind each point, read our complete thumbnail guide and the list of common thumbnail mistakes.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is my checklist saved?

    Yes, in your browser's local storage on this device, so you can come back to it. Press Reset to start a new thumbnail. Nothing is sent to us.

    What score should I aim for?

    80% or more means the fundamentals are covered. Below 50%, focus on the "fix these first" list — usually the idea, small-size readability or contrast.

    Can I use it for Shorts?

    Most points apply, but Shorts are vertical (9:16) and are mostly seen in the Shorts feed, where the opening frame matters more than the thumbnail.
