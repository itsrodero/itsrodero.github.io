The Best Free Tools for YouTube Creators

You can make professional YouTube videos without paying for software. This is the free toolkit we'd recommend to a new creator, organized by the stage of the process, with honest notes on the limits of each tool.

Every tool on this list has a genuinely free version that's good enough for regular uploads. Where a tool also has paid plans, we say so. We're not paid to include any of them.

Captions and accessibility

YouTube's automatic captions are free and generated for most videos in supported languages. Reviewing and correcting them in YouTube Studio takes a few minutes and makes your videos easier to follow for viewers who watch without sound, who are deaf or hard of hearing, or who speak another language. You can also add translated titles and descriptions in YouTube Studio.

A simple free workflow

  1. Research the topic with YouTube search suggestions, Google Trends and YouTube Studio's research data.
  2. Record with your phone or OBS; spend your effort on light and sound.
  3. Edit in DaVinci Resolve or Shotcut; clean voice audio in Audacity.
  4. Design the thumbnail in Canva, GIMP or Photopea; check it with the Thumbnail Analyzer and the feed preview.
  5. Write the title — the title generator helps with ideas and length.
  6. Publish with corrected captions, and consider a Test & Compare experiment.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need paid software to grow on YouTube?

No. The free tools above are used by many successful creators. Better ideas, clearer audio and stronger packaging matter far more than paid software.

Is music from the YouTube Audio Library really free?

Yes, for use in your YouTube videos, including monetized ones. Some tracks require attribution in the description — the library tells you which.

Are TubeBuddy or vidIQ necessary?

They can save time on keyword research, but YouTube Studio's own analytics and research data cover the essentials for free.
