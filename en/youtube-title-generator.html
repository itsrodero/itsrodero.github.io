      How to turn an idea into a great title

      Generated titles are templates. What makes a title work is the specific detail only you can add. Take "I Tried Budget Travel for 30 Days. Here's What Happened": it becomes much stronger as "I Spent 30 Days in Japan on $40 a Day. Here's What Happened" — a real place, a real number, a real promise.

      1. Pick the formula that matches the video. A tutorial fits a how-to or list; a personal story fits an experiment; a buying decision fits a comparison.
      2. Replace generic words with specifics: numbers, places, prices, names, timeframes.
      3. Put the hook in the first ~50 characters. Use the checker above to see where the title gets cut.
      4. Pair it with the thumbnail. The title should add to the image, not repeat it.
      5. Test two options with YouTube's Test & Compare when you're unsure.

      For the principles behind these formulas — including what research says about curiosity and negative framing — read how to write YouTube titles that get clicks.

      What the checker looks at

      • Length: YouTube allows 100 characters, but phones and the "Up next" column show much less. We approximate the cut at about 55 and 70 characters.
      • Capitals and punctuation: a single capitalized word can add emphasis; many capitals and repeated "!!" or "??" look spammy.
      • Emojis: one can add personality; several use up valuable space.
      • Hype words: phrases like "you won't believe" aren't banned, but they set expectations your video must meet.

      Frequently asked questions

      Does this tool use AI?

      No. It combines your topic with a curated set of title formulas, entirely in your browser. That keeps it fast and private, and it means you should always edit the result with specifics from your video.

      How long should a YouTube title be?

      Up to 100 characters are allowed. Aim to make the first 50 or so characters meaningful on their own; many good titles are 40–60 characters long.

      Should I put keywords in my title?

      Yes, if people search for the topic: use the phrase they'd type, naturally, near the start. Don't stuff multiple keywords.
