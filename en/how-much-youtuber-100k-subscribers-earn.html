How Much Does a YouTuber with 100K Subscribers Make?

There's no single answer, because YouTube doesn't pay per subscriber — it pays per view, and the value of a view varies a lot. Instead of quoting a magic number, here are transparent scenarios you can check against your own channel.

Subscribers don't pay — views do

YouTube ad revenue is earned when people watch videos with ads. A 100,000-subscriber channel that uploads twice a month to a disengaged audience might get 50,000 views a month; another channel of the same size with weekly uploads and strong search traffic might get 1,000,000. Their incomes can differ by 20 times or more.

So the useful formula is:

Monthly YouTube revenue ≈ monthly views × RPM ÷ 1,000

RPM (revenue per 1,000 views) already accounts for YouTube's share and for views without ads. If you're unsure what it is, read RPM vs. CPM explained.

What about a Shorts-first channel?

Shorts are paid from a shared Creator Pool and typically earn only cents per 1,000 views. At an illustrative $0.05 RPM, 5,000,000 monthly Shorts views would bring in about $250. Many Shorts-focused creators reach 100K subscribers quickly but earn far less from YouTube than long-form creators of the same size — which is why they often rely on sponsorships or use Shorts to grow a long-form channel. Also note that from February 1, 2027, earning from the Shorts Creator Pool requires maintaining 10 million qualified Shorts views over the previous 90 days (see monetization requirements).

What moves a 100K channel up or down the table

  • Upload consistency: most channels get a large share of their monthly views from recent uploads.
  • Back catalog: evergreen videos that keep getting found through search add steady views every month.
  • Audience location: viewers in higher-paying ad markets raise RPM.
  • Video length: videos of 8+ minutes can include mid-roll ads.
  • Season: Q4 usually pays more; January usually pays less.
  • Advertiser-friendliness: limited ads can cut a video's earnings sharply.

In the US, sponsorship and affiliate income must be disclosed to viewers (the FTC's endorsement guides), and YouTube asks you to tick the paid promotion box when a video includes a sponsorship.

Don't forget costs and taxes

Revenue isn't profit. Equipment, software, editing help, music licenses and travel all come out of it, and YouTube income is taxable. YouTube (via AdSense) also collects tax information from every creator and may withhold US taxes on earnings from US viewers, depending on your country and tax forms.

Estimate your own numbers

The fastest way to a realistic estimate is to take your channel's actual RPM from YouTube Studio (Analytics → Revenue) and multiply by the views you expect. Our YouTube revenue calculator does this for daily, monthly and yearly views, with separate inputs for Shorts.

Frequently asked questions

Does YouTube pay a bonus at 100,000 subscribers?

No. YouTube sends the Silver Creator Award plaque to eligible channels that reach 100,000 subscribers, but there's no cash bonus. Income depends on views and other revenue sources.

Can you live off 100K subscribers?

Some creators can, especially with steady views, a higher-RPM niche and sponsorships. Many can't from AdSense alone. The scenarios above show how wide the range is.

How often does YouTube pay?

Through AdSense for YouTube, monthly, once your balance passes the payment threshold (US$100 for accounts paid in dollars) and your payment details are complete.
