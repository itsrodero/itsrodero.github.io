How the thumbnail downloader works

Every public YouTube video has a set of thumbnail images stored on YouTube's image servers (i.ytimg.com). When you paste a link, TubeTools reads the 11-character video ID from it and loads those images directly from YouTube into your browser. We don't run a server in the middle, so the link you paste isn't sent to us or stored anywhere except your own browser's "Recent" list, which you can clear at any time.

Which sizes can you get?

YouTube generates the same five files for most videos. The largest one is usually only created for videos uploaded in HD, so older or low-resolution videos may top out at 640×480.

Creators can now upload thumbnails up to 3840×2160 in YouTube Studio, but these public image URLs still deliver the sizes above. If you need the original file of your own video, keep a copy of your design file — YouTube doesn't give the original back.

Why would you download a thumbnail?

  • Research your niche. Put the top videos for a topic side by side and look for patterns in color, faces, text and framing. Our guide on analyzing competitor thumbnails walks through a simple method.
  • Check your own work. Download your published thumbnail and run it through the Thumbnail Analyzer or the feed preview to see how it holds up at small sizes.
  • Commentary, reviews and teaching. Showing a thumbnail while you talk about the video is a common, legitimate use.
  • Recover a lost file. If you deleted the design file for one of your own videos, the maximum-resolution version is the best copy YouTube keeps.

Is it legal to download YouTube thumbnails?

Viewing and saving a publicly available image for personal reference is generally fine, but thumbnails are creative works protected by copyright, and the rights belong to the channel that made them. Don't re-upload someone else's thumbnail as your own, use it in ads, or sell it. For commentary, criticism or education, fair use (US) or quotation rights (EU) may apply, but they depend on the specific case. When in doubt, ask the creator. This isn't legal advice.

Troubleshooting

  • Only small sizes appear: the video doesn't have a 1280×720 version. That's a YouTube limitation.
  • No title shows up: the video may be private, age-restricted or deleted. Thumbnails can still exist for a while after a video is removed.
  • The download button opens a new tab instead: some browsers block programmatic downloads. Right-click the image and choose "Save image as…".
