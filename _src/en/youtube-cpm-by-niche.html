Key takeaways

  • Niches close to expensive purchases (finance, real estate, law, software) usually earn the most per view.
  • Audience country matters as much as niche: in one 2026 dataset, a view from Spain earned about 30% of a US view, and a view from Mexico about 5%.
  • Real channel data is lower and more spread out than most published tables, so treat any range as a rough guide.
  • Views usually matter more than niche. Choose a topic you can sustain.

Why niches earn different amounts

YouTube sells ad space through auctions. The price depends on how many advertisers want to reach a particular viewer watching a particular kind of video, and on how much a customer is worth to them. That's why some topics consistently attract higher bids:

  • Value of the customer. A bank, broker or software company may earn hundreds or thousands of dollars from a new customer, so it can afford to pay more per ad. A mobile game or snack brand earns less per customer.
  • Purchase intent. Viewers watching reviews, comparisons and "how to start" videos are closer to buying something.
  • Audience location. Ads aimed at viewers in the US, Canada, Australia, the UK and the Nordic countries usually cost the most (see RPM by country below).
  • Audience age and ad eligibility. Content made for kids can't show personalized ads, and sensitive topics may get limited ads.

What real channel data shows

Most tables online, including the one above, are compiled from what creators say. One of the few sources that publishes figures taken from YouTube Studio is AIR Media-Tech, which works with YouTube channels. Its 2026 study used 300 channels and 3,595 monetized channel-months from May 2025 to May 2026, and reports medians. The sample isn't limited to US audiences, so its numbers are lower than US-heavy ranges. Even so, the pattern is useful:

  • Education & Science had the highest median RPM, $10.22. Kids & Teens had the lowest, $0.33.
  • Most other categories clustered around $2–$3: Lifestyle $2.98, Entertainment $2.43, Gadgets & Tech $2.33, Music $2.28 and Gaming $2.05. Business & Finance came in at $2.01, but from fewer than ten channels.
  • The spread inside a niche is huge. In Education & Science, the middle half of channels earned between $2.31 and $19.50 per 1,000 views.

The lesson: two channels in the same niche can differ more than two different niches do. Where your viewers live and what kind of videos you make within the niche decide much of your RPM.

What about Spanish-language channels?

A Spanish-language channel usually has a mixed audience: Mexico, Spain, the United States, Colombia, Argentina and the rest of Latin America. Viewers in Spain and Spanish speakers in the US bring in more per view; viewers in Latin America bring in much less. In the dataset above, a view from Spain was worth about 29% of a US view, and views from Mexico, Colombia or Argentina 3–5%. For a typical mix, our calculator uses 15% of the US-heavy range. Your real figure depends on your own split, which YouTube Studio shows under Analytics → Revenue, by geography.

What really decides your RPM inside a niche

Niche sets the rough range, but your own numbers depend on choices you make:

  • Commercial topics within your niche: a gaming channel's hardware reviews usually earn more than its let's-plays.
  • Video length: videos of 8 minutes or longer can include mid-roll ads.
  • Advertiser-friendliness: limited ads can cut revenue dramatically.
  • Season: ad demand rises in Q4 and dips in January.
  • Format mix: more Shorts lowers overall RPM.
  • Audience location: the same niche can earn several times more with a US-heavy audience.

How to choose a niche (without chasing CPM)

A high-CPM niche is worthless if you can't make videos people want to watch, week after week. A better way to decide is to score a few candidate niches on these questions:

QuestionWhy it matters
Can you make 50 videos on this without running out of ideas or interest?Consistency matters more than any single video.
Do you have real knowledge, access or experience others don't?It's your edge against established channels, and it's what viewers trust.
Are people searching for and watching this topic?Demand decides your view ceiling. Check YouTube search suggestions and competitors' view counts.
Can you stand out from the existing channels?A crowded niche is fine if you have a different angle, format or audience.
Are there income options beyond ads?Sponsorships, affiliate programs and products often pay more than AdSense in any niche.
What's the likely RPM range?Useful as a tiebreaker, not as the main reason.
A simple sanity checkRevenue is views × RPM. A gaming channel with 1,000,000 monthly views at $2 RPM earns about $2,000; a finance channel with 100,000 monthly views at $15 RPM earns about $1,500. Frequently asked questions

What is the highest-paying YouTube niche?

Personal finance, investing, real estate, law and business/software topics are usually reported as the highest-paying for English-language, US-heavy audiences, because advertisers in those industries pay more per customer. In real channel data, education channels also do very well.

Which country has the highest YouTube RPM?

In Dynamoi's 2026 dataset, Australia ($7.12), Norway ($6.38), the US ($5.14) and Canada ($5.09) were at the top, with the UK close behind. Rankings vary between datasets, but the same high-income English-speaking and Nordic markets are always near the top.

How much is a view from Spain or Mexico worth compared with the US?

In the same dataset, a view from Spain earned about 29% of a US view and a view from Mexico about 5%. Your niche and video length still matter, so check your own revenue by geography in YouTube Studio.

Should I switch niches to earn more?

Usually not. Switching resets your audience and your advantage. A better approach is to add higher-value topics that fit your current audience, such as reviews, buying guides or tutorials.

Why is my RPM different from these ranges?

Audience location, format, video length, season and ad eligibility all shift RPM. These ranges are rough comparisons, not benchmarks for a specific channel.
