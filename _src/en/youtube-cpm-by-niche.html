Why niches earn different amounts

YouTube sells ad space through auctions. The price depends on how many advertisers want to reach a particular viewer watching a particular kind of video, and on how much a customer is worth to them. That's why some topics consistently attract higher bids:

  • Value of the customer. A bank, broker or software company may earn hundreds or thousands of dollars from a new customer, so it can afford to pay more per ad. A mobile game or snack brand earns less per customer.
  • Purchase intent. Viewers watching reviews, comparisons and "how to start" videos are closer to buying something.
  • Audience location. Ads aimed at viewers in the US, Canada, Australia, the UK and parts of Western Europe usually cost more.
  • Audience age and ad eligibility. Content made for kids can't show personalized ads, and sensitive topics may get limited ads.

YouTube doesn't publish revenue data by niche, so any table like this is an estimate. The ranges below summarize what English-language creators with mostly US audiences commonly report for long-form videos. Treat them as orders of magnitude to compare niches, not as a prediction for your channel — individual channels regularly fall outside them.

What about Spanish-language and other audiences?

The same niche can earn noticeably less when most viewers are outside the highest-paying markets. For Spanish-language channels, audiences in Spain and the United States usually generate more ad revenue per view than audiences in many Latin American countries. If you create in more than one language, check your revenue by geography in YouTube Studio to see how this plays out for you.

What really decides your RPM inside a niche

Niche sets the rough range, but your own numbers depend on choices you make:

  • Commercial topics within your niche: a gaming channel's hardware reviews usually earn more than its let's-plays.
  • Video length: videos of 8 minutes or longer can include mid-roll ads.
  • Advertiser-friendliness: limited ads can cut revenue dramatically.
  • Season: ad demand rises in Q4 and dips in January.
  • Format mix: more Shorts lowers overall RPM.

How to choose a niche (without chasing CPM)

A high-CPM niche is worthless if you can't make videos people want to watch, week after week. A better way to decide is to score a few candidate niches on these questions:

QuestionWhy it matters
Can you make 50 videos on this without running out of ideas or interest?Consistency matters more than any single video.
Do you have real knowledge, access or experience others don't?It's your edge against established channels, and it's what viewers trust.
Are people searching for and watching this topic?Demand decides your view ceiling. Check YouTube search suggestions and competitors' view counts.
Can you stand out from the existing channels?A crowded niche is fine if you have a different angle, format or audience.
Are there income options beyond ads?Sponsorships, affiliate programs and products often pay more than AdSense in any niche.
What's the likely RPM range?Useful as a tiebreaker, not as the main reason.
A simple sanity checkRevenue is views × RPM. A gaming channel with 1,000,000 monthly views at $2 RPM earns about $2,000; a finance channel with 100,000 monthly views at $15 RPM earns about $1,500. Views usually matter more than niche. Model your own scenarios with the revenue calculator.

Frequently asked questions

What is the highest-paying YouTube niche?

Personal finance, investing and business/software topics are usually reported as the highest-paying for English-language, US-heavy audiences, because advertisers in those industries pay more per customer.

Should I switch niches to earn more?

Usually not. Switching resets your audience and your advantage. A better approach is to add higher-value topics that fit your current audience, such as reviews, buying guides or tutorials.

Why is my RPM different from these ranges?

Audience location, format, video length, season and ad eligibility all shift RPM. These ranges are rough comparisons, not benchmarks for a specific channel.
