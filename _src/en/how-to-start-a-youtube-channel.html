Key takeaways

  • Decide who the channel is for and what you'll make before the name and branding.
  • Verify your phone number right away: it unlocks custom thumbnails, longer uploads and live streaming.
  • Your first ten videos are practice. Publish consistently, study the analytics, and improve one thing at a time.

Step 1: Decide what the channel is about

The most useful thing you can do before creating anything is to write one sentence: "This channel helps [who] with [what]." For example: "This channel helps beginners cook cheap weeknight meals." That sentence will guide your name, your banner, your first video ideas and even which viewers YouTube recommends you to.

  • Pick something you can make 50 videos about without running out of ideas or interest.
  • Check there's demand: type your topic into YouTube search and look at the suggestions and at how many views recent videos get.
  • Find your angle: what can you show, explain or do differently from the channels already there?

If income is one of your goals, read RPM by niche — but choose a topic you can sustain first. Views matter more than niche.

Step 2: Create the channel

  1. Sign in to YouTube with a Google Account.
  2. Open your profile picture menu → Create a channel.
  3. Choose a name and a handle (the @name people can mention and search). You can change both later, within limits.

If several people will manage the channel, or you want to keep it separate from your personal Google account, you can create the channel under a Brand Account so you can add managers without sharing your password.

Step 3: Verify your account

In YouTube Studio, go to Settings → Channel → Feature eligibility and verify your phone number. This unlocks intermediate features such as custom thumbnails, videos longer than 15 minutes and live streaming. Advanced features (like Test & Compare) come later with channel history or ID/video verification.

Step 4: Set up the channel page

ElementRecommendation
Profile pictureSquare image around 800 × 800 px; it's shown as a small circle, so keep it simple — a face or a bold logo.
BannerAt least 2048 × 1152 px, with text inside the central safe area. See the banner size guide and banner checker.
DescriptionTwo or three sentences saying who the channel is for, what you post and how often. Use the words people would search.
LinksYour website or main social profile, and a contact email for business enquiries if you want sponsorships.

Step 5: Set upload defaults

In YouTube Studio → Settings → Upload defaults you can pre-fill things you repeat on every video: a description template (with your links), the category, the language and comment settings. It saves time and keeps every video consistent.

You'll also be asked whether your content is made for kids. Answer accurately — it's a legal requirement in many countries, and it changes which features and ads are available.

Step 6: Get simple, good-enough gear

  • Camera: a modern phone is enough for most channels.
  • Sound: the most important upgrade. An inexpensive clip-on or USB microphone makes a bigger difference than a new camera.
  • Light: face a window, or use one soft light in front of you.
  • Editing and design software: free options are excellent — see the best free tools for creators.

Step 7: Make your first videos

  1. Plan a list of 10 video ideas within your focus, favoring questions people search for.
  2. Open strong: show what the viewer will get in the first seconds; cut long intros.
  3. Package each video carefully: one clear idea for the thumbnail and a specific title. Use the thumbnail checklist, the title generator and the feed preview.
  4. Write a useful description with chapters — the chapters generator formats them for you.
  5. Publish on a schedule you can keep, even if it's once every two weeks.

Step 8: Learn from your analytics

After a handful of videos, YouTube Studio starts to tell you useful things. The three numbers to watch first:

  • Impressions click-through rate: is the packaging working? See CTR explained.
  • Average view duration and retention: is the video keeping the promise? See CTR vs. watch time.
  • Traffic sources: are people finding you through search, suggestions or browse? See YouTube SEO.

Step 9: Follow the rules from day one

  • Read YouTube's Community Guidelines and copyright basics. Don't use music, clips or images you don't have rights to.
  • Never buy views or subscribers — it can get the channel terminated and blocks monetization.
  • Keep titles and thumbnails honest: misleading packaging breaks the spam policies and loses viewers.

Step 10: Plan the path to monetization

You can earn from fan funding and shopping at 500 subscribers, and from ads at 1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 watch hours — rising to 8,000 hours for new applicants from February 1, 2027. See the full monetization requirements and track your progress with the watch hours calculator.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to start a YouTube channel?

Nothing. Creating a channel is free, and a phone plus free editing software is enough to start.

How old do you have to be?

You must meet the minimum age to have a Google Account in your country (13 in most countries). To earn money through AdSense you must be 18, or use a parent's or guardian's account.

How often should a new channel upload?

As often as you can while keeping quality — consistency matters more than volume. One good video a week is a strong schedule for most new creators.
