Key takeaways

  • Find it in YouTube Studio → Analytics; use Advanced mode to compare videos and date ranges.
  • Two questions explain most results: do people click? (impressions and CTR) and do they keep watching? (average view duration and retention).
  • YouTube says half of all channels and videos have an impressions CTR between 2% and 10% — compare each video with your own channel's typical numbers, not with other creators.

Where to find YouTube Analytics

  1. Open YouTube Studio (studio.youtube.com) or the YouTube Studio app.
  2. Select Analytics in the left menu for your whole channel, or open one video under Content and select Analytics for that video.
  3. Use the tabs — such as Overview, Content, Audience and Trends, plus Revenue if you're in the YouTube Partner Program.
  4. Select Advanced mode (top right) for detailed tables, filters and comparisons between videos or periods.

The Realtime card shows estimated views from the last 48 hours and the last 60 minutes, which is handy right after you publish. Other reports can take a day or two to settle, so don't judge a video on its first few hours.

Impressions and CTR: do people click?

An impression is counted when your thumbnail is shown to someone on YouTube — on the home page, in search, in suggested videos and so on. Impressions from outside YouTube, such as embeds on other sites, and end screens aren't counted, which is why views can be higher than impressions for videos shared a lot outside YouTube.

Impressions CTR is the share of those impressions that led to a view. YouTube's own figure is that half of all channels and videos have an impressions CTR between 2% and 10%, and new channels and videos with few views can swing much more. CTR also tends to drop as a video is shown to more people, especially on the home page, so a falling CTR with rising impressions is normal.

A low CTR with healthy impressions usually points to the thumbnail or title. Check them at real size with our thumbnail preview tool and read the CTR guide for beginners.

Retention: do people keep watching?

Open a video's analytics and look at the audience retention graph. It shows how much of the audience is still watching at each moment. Three patterns to look for:

  • A steep drop in the first 30 seconds: the start doesn't match what the title and thumbnail promised, or it takes too long to get going.
  • Spikes: people rewatching or skipping to a part — often the most valuable moment of the video.
  • A slow, steady decline: normal. Compare the curve with your other videos rather than aiming for a flat line.

Click-through and retention work together: a high CTR with poor retention means the packaging overpromised. Read CTR vs. watch time for how YouTube weighs the two.

Traffic sources: how people find you

  • Browse features — the home page and subscription feed. Growth here means YouTube is recommending you widely.
  • Suggested videos — shown next to or after other videos. Strong for channels whose videos fit well with popular ones in the niche.
  • YouTube search — viewers searching for a topic. The report lists the search terms, which are great ideas for titles and new videos; see the YouTube SEO guide.
  • External — websites, apps and social networks.
  • Shorts feed, channel pages, playlists and notifications — smaller, but they show how your existing audience reaches you.

Audience: who's watching and when

The Audience tab shows returning and new viewers, unique viewers, subscriber watch time and a heatmap of when your viewers are on YouTube. That heatmap is the best guide to when to publish — more in the best time to post on YouTube. The tab also lists other channels and videos your audience watches, which is useful for spotting topics and formats that work in your niche.

Shorts: views vs. engaged views

Since March 31, 2025, a Shorts view is counted every time a Short starts to play or replay. The older way of counting is still available as engaged views in Advanced mode, and that's what YouTube Partner Program eligibility and Shorts revenue are based on. When you compare Shorts from before and after that date, use engaged views.

A 10-minute weekly routine

  1. Overview, last 28 days: are views, watch time and subscribers going up or down compared with the previous period?
  2. Your latest video: compare its first 7 days with your typical video in Advanced mode — impressions, CTR and average view duration.
  3. Retention graph: note where the biggest drop happens and fix that part in the next video.
  4. Search terms: write down two or three search terms worth a video of their own.
  5. Watch hours: if you're working toward monetization, check your progress with the watch hours calculator.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good CTR on YouTube?

There's no single number. YouTube says half of channels and videos fall between 2% and 10%. The useful comparison is with your own channel's recent videos shown to similar audiences.

Why are my impressions low?

YouTube is showing the video to few people, often because it's new, the channel is small, or early viewers didn't click or keep watching. Improving the thumbnail, title and first 30 seconds is the most direct fix.

Why don't my views match my impressions?

Not every view comes from a counted impression: views from external sites, end screens and some other places don't have one. So views ÷ impressions won't equal the reported CTR.

How long does YouTube Analytics take to update?

Realtime estimates update within minutes; most other reports can take a day or two to be complete.
