    What the analyzer checks — and why

    Most thumbnails are seen at a fraction of their designed size, often on a phone, next to many other thumbnails. The analyzer focuses on measurable properties that affect how readable your image is in that situation. It doesn't try to predict your click-through rate: no tool can do that from the image alone, because topic, title and audience matter just as much.

    Technical checks (uploaded files)

    • Aspect ratio: YouTube thumbnails for regular videos should be exactly 16:9. Anything else is letterboxed or cropped.
    • Resolution: YouTube requires at least 640 pixels wide and now recommends up to 3840×2160. 1280×720 or larger looks sharp in most places.
    • File size: the upload limit is 50 MB from a computer but 2 MB from the mobile app.

    Brightness and contrast

    Brightness is the average perceived lightness of the image. Contrast is measured as how much lightness varies across the image (the standard deviation of perceived lightness). Low contrast is the most common reason a thumbnail looks flat and muddy at small sizes. The grayscale preview is the quickest way to judge it by eye: if your subject doesn't stand out in black and white, it won't stand out in color either. Our color theory guide explains how to fix it.

    Colorfulness

    We use a colorfulness measure published by Hasler and Süsstrunk (2003), which correlates well with how colorful people judge an image to be. Their scale runs from "not colorful" through "moderately" and "quite" to "extremely colorful". Muted thumbnails can work as a deliberate style, but they need strong brightness contrast to stand out.

    Visual clutter

    Clutter is estimated from edge density: the share of the image with strong changes in lightness. Many edges usually means many small details, which turn into noise at feed size. It's a rough proxy, so use it together with your own eyes and the small-size previews.

    Edges and the bottom-right corner

    YouTube displays thumbnails on a white background in light mode and a near-black one in dark mode. If the outer edge of your image is very dark or very light, it can blend into one of them. The analyzer also looks for detail in the bottom-right corner, which is covered by the video-length badge.

    Privacy

    Your image is processed entirely on your device with the browser's Canvas API. When you analyze a YouTube link, the thumbnail is loaded directly from YouTube's image server. Nothing is sent to TubeTools.

    Frequently asked questions

    Does a good score mean my video will get more clicks?

    No. The checks remove common technical weaknesses, but clicks depend on the idea, the title, the audience and competition. Use YouTube's Test & Compare to measure real performance — see our A/B testing guide.

    Why is my deliberately dark thumbnail flagged?

    Dark styles can work well, but dark edges tend to disappear in dark mode. The warning is a reminder to check, not a rule — look at the dark-mode preview and decide.

    Can I analyze competitors' thumbnails?

    Yes: paste a video link. It's a useful way to compare your design with what already ranks for your topic. The images belong to their creators, so use them for analysis only.
