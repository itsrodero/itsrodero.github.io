Key takeaways

  • Shorts feed ads go into a Creator Pool; you get 45% of the part allocated to you.
  • Your allocation depends on your share of engaged views among monetizing creators — not on the ads shown on your Shorts.
  • From February 1, 2027, earning from the pool requires 10 million qualified Shorts views in the previous 90 days.
  • Shorts usually earn far less per view than long-form videos; many creators use them mainly to grow.

Why Shorts are paid differently

On a regular video, an ad plays before or during your video, so the revenue clearly belongs to you. In the Shorts feed, viewers swipe between many Shorts from different creators and ads appear between them. There's no single video an ad belongs to. So YouTube pools the revenue and splits it.

How the Creator Pool works, step by step

  1. Ads run in the Shorts feed. Revenue from those ads is added together each month (per country).
  2. Music costs are separated out. Part of the revenue is set aside to pay for music used in Shorts. If a Short uses one track, half of the revenue associated with its views goes to music rights holders; with two tracks, about two thirds.
  3. The rest forms the Creator Pool for monetizing creators.
  4. The pool is split by engaged views. If your Shorts earned 1% of all eligible engaged views from monetizing creators in a country, you're allocated 1% of that country's pool.
  5. You receive 45% of the revenue allocated to you. YouTube keeps 55%.
Music doesn't reduce your personal shareAccording to YouTube, your allocation from the Creator Pool isn't affected by whether you use music. Music affects how big the overall pool is, because it's calculated across all Shorts.

What counts as a view for Shorts

Shorts analytics distinguish between all views and engaged views, which filter out very short or repeated plays. Revenue sharing uses engaged views, and Shorts RPM is calculated per 1,000 engaged views. That's why a Short can show many more views than the number used for revenue.

How much do Shorts pay?

YouTube doesn't publish a fixed rate, and results vary a lot by audience country and season. What creators commonly report is that Shorts earn a small fraction of what long-form videos earn per view — often a few cents per 1,000 views. As a rough illustration only:

Who can earn from Shorts

  • Join the Partner Program through either path: 1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 public watch hours (12 months) or 10 million public Shorts views (90 days). For new applicants from February 1, 2027, these become 8,000 hours or 20 million Shorts views.
  • Accept the Shorts Monetization Module in YouTube Studio. You only earn from Shorts views that happen after you accept it.
  • From February 1, 2027: maintain 10 million qualified Shorts views over the previous 90 days to earn from the pool each month. Falling below doesn't remove you from the Partner Program or affect long-form earnings; it pauses Shorts pool earnings until you're back above it.

Estimate how far you are with the watch hours calculator, which also checks the Shorts thresholds. Full details are in YouTube monetization requirements.

What isn't eligible

  • Shorts that reuse content without meaningful original contribution (for example unedited clips of other people's videos, or compilations).
  • Shorts with artificial views.
  • Content that isn't advertiser-friendly or that breaks the Community Guidelines.

Making Shorts work for your channel

Because Shorts pay relatively little per view, it helps to judge them by what they do for the channel as a whole:

  • Discovery: Shorts can put your channel in front of many new people quickly.
  • Funnel to long-form: link to a related long video (YouTube lets you add a related video to a Short) and make the Short a genuine teaser.
  • Consistency without burnout: repurposing the best moments of your long videos into Shorts keeps your channel active, which also helps meet the 2027 activity requirements.
  • Openings matter most: in the swipe feed, the first second does the job a thumbnail does for long videos.

Frequently asked questions

Do Shorts views count toward the 4,000 watch hours?

No. Watch time from Shorts in the Shorts feed doesn't count toward long-form watch hours. Shorts have their own eligibility path based on Shorts views.

Why is my Shorts RPM so much lower than my long-form RPM?

Shorts revenue is shared across a huge number of views, part of it pays for music, and creators receive 45% rather than the 55% share on long-form ads.

Will I lose Shorts income in 2027?

Only in months where your channel has fewer than 10 million qualified Shorts views over the previous 90 days. Your other YPP earnings aren't affected.
