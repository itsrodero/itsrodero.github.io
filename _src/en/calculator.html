How the calculator works

YouTube reports your RPM (revenue per 1,000 views) after its revenue share, across all views, including ones without ads. That makes the math simple:

Estimated revenue = views × RPM ÷ 1,000

If you choose a niche, the calculator starts from an indicative RPM range for that niche with a mostly US audience, the same ranges as our RPM by niche and country guide. It then scales the range by where most of your viewers are. A view from Western Europe, for example, is counted at 35% of a US view, and one from Latin America at 10%. These factors are rounded from a public 2026 dataset of creator RPM by country, and the guide shows the underlying numbers. The niche ranges come from figures creators commonly share publicly; neither is official YouTube data.

If your channel is monetized, switch to "Use my own RPM" and enter the number from YouTube Studio. It already includes your niche, countries and formats, so it will always be more accurate than any average.

Example: an education channel with 100,000 long-form views a month
  • US-heavy audience: $3–$10 RPM, so about $300–$1,000 a month.
  • Mostly Western Europe: $3–$10 × 35% = $1.05–$3.50 RPM, so about $105–$350 a month.
  • Spanish-speaking mix: $3–$10 × 15% = $0.45–$1.50 RPM, so about $45–$150 a month.

Shorts are calculated separately because they're paid from a shared pool and usually earn only cents per 1,000 views. The default Shorts RPM is an illustrative value; replace it with your own.

Why estimates can be off

  • Audience location: the country factors are averages for whole regions. Your real mix of countries, which YouTube Studio shows under Revenue by geography, decides your actual figure.
  • Season: RPM usually rises in October–December and drops in January.
  • Video length: videos of 8 minutes or more can include mid-roll ads.
  • Limited ads: videos that aren't fully advertiser-friendly earn much less.
  • Other revenue: memberships, Supers and YouTube Premium are included in your real RPM but vary a lot between channels.

To understand these factors, read RPM vs. CPM explained, RPM by niche and how to increase your RPM.

Frequently asked questions

How much does YouTube pay per 1,000 views?

That's your RPM, and it varies widely by niche, audience location and format — from well under $1 for many Shorts to $20 or more for some finance and business channels. Your own YouTube Studio shows your exact figure.

How much does YouTube pay per 1,000 views in Spain or Mexico?

Much less than in the US. In a public 2026 dataset, creators earned about $1.50 per 1,000 views from Spain and about $0.27 from Mexico, against about $5.14 from the US. Your niche can move these numbers up or down.

How much is 1 million views worth?

At an RPM of $2, about $2,000; at $5, about $5,000; at $12, about $12,000. For Shorts, 1 million views typically earns far less.

Is my data saved?

Your inputs are remembered in your browser so they're there next time. Nothing is sent to us.
